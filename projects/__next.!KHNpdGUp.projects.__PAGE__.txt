15:T692,## What it does

Watches the live Twitter stream for tweets carrying Spotify links, counts each track share by artist, and keeps a rolling top-20 chart current. The chart re-renders every five seconds and covers the last 60 minutes.

## The pipeline

A Tweepy listener filters the stream twice: the API-side keyword "spotify com" so tweets carry a Spotify link, then a JSON check that keeps only tweets with a URL, extracting just the timestamp and the expanded link. Those records land on a Kafka topic as small JSON messages. A PySpark consumer reads the topic receiver-less, querying Kafka directly for the latest offsets, pulls the track id out of each URL, and resolves it to an artist through the Spotify Web API - records with no match are dropped. Counts are written to Cassandra as (artist, timestamp, count) rows. A Dash app re-queries the table every five seconds, aggregates the trailing hour, and redraws the chart.

![Illustrative pipeline diagram: tweets filtered by Tweepy into Kafka, Spark Streaming resolves artists through the Spotify API, counts land in Cassandra, and a Dash chart refreshes every five seconds](/assets/images/projects/streaming-pipeline.svg)

## What rereading it years later exposed

Two quiet failures: the consumer never saved its restart offsets, so any crash replayed the whole topic, and the Cassandra rows could collide on a shared primary key, so one artist's update could overwrite another's. Both are written up in detail - the [write-up](/posts/building-a-streaming-pipeline/) walks through what the original README never said. ## Data and cleaning

Two public datasets: 1.6 million tweets from Sentiment140 and, for contrast, Amazon product reviews with longer, richer text. Cleaning was its own experiment because the representation mattered as much as the classifier - stripping usernames, URLs and noise, mapping contractions, correcting spelling, removing stopwords, and lemmatizing with NLTK. Trigram TF-IDF features beat a plain count vectorizer. The cleaned datasets are published on Kaggle.

## What the numbers said, and what they did not

The linear SVM came out best: 82% accuracy, 86% AUC. The CNN reached 0.777 on the held-out set. The same pipeline scored above 90% on the Amazon reviews, which says more about longer texts than about the models. Four components, as in the source papers. An encoder extracts mid-level features from the grayscale input while a pretrained Inception-ResNet-v2 supplies high-level scene features; a fusion step injects those into the middle of the network, and a decoder predicts the a* and b* channels of the Lab colour space, which recombine with the input's luminance into the final image.
