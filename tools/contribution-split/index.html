Why can a share drop when I add money?

A share can drop even when money is added, because the total grows too. The rule tops up the holding furthest below its target, relative to that target, until it draws level with the next one. The biggest gap from target shrinks overall, but not every holding improves: with a small deposit a holding can end slightly further from its target.

How it is calculated

Fill the largest relative shortfall first. Each target becomes a weight w = target ÷ sum of targets. Money to add to a holding is A = max(0, w × L − V), where V is its current worth and L is one common funding level, found by narrowing a range until the amounts add up to your contribution C. A holding with target 0 gets nothing. Nothing is sold.

For Holding B: w = 0,300, V = USD 2.100, L ≈ 8.600,00. L is a funding level used by the rule, not your portfolio total.

Share after = (V + A) ÷ (total worth + C). Biggest gap = largest absolute difference between a share and its target, in points: yours goes from 12,0 to 9,0. Amounts are rounded to whole units and any rounding remainder goes to the largest amount. This is one explainable rule, not the only possible one.

Assumptions and limits
  • Only new money moves. A holding above its target simply gets nothing this time.
  • Targets that do not add up to 100% are treated as shares of their sum, or scale them with one tap.
  • Taxes, fees, minimum order sizes and market prices are not modelled. Worth is what you type.
  • Your numbers stay in this browser and are never sent. Not financial advice.
