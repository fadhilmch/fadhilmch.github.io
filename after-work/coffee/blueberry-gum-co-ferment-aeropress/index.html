Blueberry Gum co-ferment AeroPress, no-bypass clarity brew

  • aeropress
  • indonesia
  • co-ferment
Coffee
Water
250g
Ratio
1:16.7
Temp
93°C
Time
2:00-2:30
Grind
medium-fine (Silberthal 3.5)
Beans
Blueberry Gum, Java Arabika (natural co-ferment, 1200-1300 MASL)
Roastery
Hanggar

Brew

0:00 of 2:30Step 2
  1. 0:00 Setup: set the Silberthal to scale 3.5 (medium-fine) and grind 15g fresh. Place a paper filter in the AeroPress cap, rinse with hot water over your mug to remove the papery taste and preheat the vessel. Discard the rinse water and assemble the AeroPress in the standard upright position on your mug.
  2. 0:00 Bloom: add the grounds and level the bed with a gentle shake. Pour 40g of the 250g water at 92-94°C, ensuring full saturation. Swirl the chamber once gently, do not stir. Bloom for 35 seconds.
  3. 0:35 Main pour: slowly and steadily pour the remaining water in a gentle circular motion to reach 250g total, finishing the pour by 1:00. Do not stir; keep the pour low and gentle to minimise agitation.
  4. 1:00 Steep: leave undisturbed until 2:00 total brew time. No stirring; this protects the volatile blueberry and fermented-fruit esters of the co-ferment bean.
  5. 2:00 Press: insert the plunger and press very slowly and steadily for a full 30 seconds. Stop immediately at the first hiss, do not press through it. A slow, controlled press is the most important step for cup clarity.
  6. ~2:30 Serve: remove the AeroPress and serve immediately. Taste the cup black first; a well-dialled co-ferment blueberry should show clean blueberry sweetness, a wine-like acidity and a smooth, round finish with no harsh bitterness.

Notes

Silberthal setting: start at scale 3.5 (medium-fine). Go finer toward 3 if the cup tastes sour or thin, coarser toward 4 if bitter or over-extracted. Why this works for co-ferment blueberry: 92-94°C is slightly off boil to protect the delicate fruity acids and fermentation esters; low agitation (no stirring after the bloom, only a gentle swirl) preserves aromatic clarity; a paper filter (Aesir recommended) removes oils and fines that muddy fruit-forward flavors; and the slow press is the single biggest clarity factor, since hard pressing creates turbulence and a muddy cup. The standard non-inverted method is used because the full 250g fits cleanly; switch to inverted if you notice pre-drip during the steep.
