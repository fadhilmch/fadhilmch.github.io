20:T5a4,<p>A reranker reads the query and one candidate together and scores the pair. That is more accurate than comparing two separately computed embeddings, and much slower, so it only runs on the top N of a first search. Azure’s semantic ranker takes the top 50 and scores each from 0 (irrelevant) to 4 (answers it completely).</p>
<p>Decision rules:</p>
<ul>
<li>It can only reorder. A chunk outside the window stays missed, so recall comes from the first stage (<a href="/notes/hybrid-search/">[[hybrid-search]]</a>).</li>
<li>Judge it by ranking (MRR, the right chunk nearer the top), not recall. Recall often barely moves.</li>
<li>Its score has a fixed meaning, unlike a <a href="/notes/reciprocal-rank-fusion/">[[reciprocal-rank-fusion]]</a> score, so this is the score to threshold. Keep thresholds coarse; the score distribution shifts a little with model updates.</li>
<li>Check it per language and per slice on a <a href="/notes/golden-dataset/">[[golden-dataset]]</a>. A multilingual reranker can be weaker in some languages while the average looks fine (<a href="/notes/evals/">[[evals]]</a>).</li>
</ul>
