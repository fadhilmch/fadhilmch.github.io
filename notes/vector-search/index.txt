20:T5dc,<p>An embedding model maps text to a vector, and search returns the chunks nearest to the query vector, usually by cosine similarity. Engines use approximate nearest neighbour search, most often HNSW: a stack of graphs searched from the sparse top layer down, which scales well but now and then misses the true nearest chunk.</p>
<p>Good at paraphrases, synonyms and, with a multilingual model, questions in a different language from the document. Weak on exact tokens: error codes, product names, internal acronyms, numbers (806 and 809 can land close together). That is why it usually runs next to <a href="/notes/bm25/">[[bm25]]</a> as <a href="/notes/hybrid-search/">[[hybrid-search]]</a>, not instead of it.</p>
<p>Defaults and gotchas:</p>
<ul>
<li>An off-the-shelf model on company documents is out of domain. BEIR found BM25 a strong baseline there, so measure before dropping keyword search.</li>
<li>Ask for more neighbours (k around 50) than you keep. A <a href="/notes/reranking/">[[reranking]]</a> step or a threshold trims later.</li>
<li>With a filter, prefiltering keeps k results if they exist; postfiltering can return fewer (<a href="/notes/metadata-filters/">[[metadata-filters]]</a>).</li>
<li>Changing the embedding model means re-embedding the whole index. Vectors from two models don’t mix.</li>
